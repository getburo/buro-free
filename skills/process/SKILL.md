# Buro · Process — quality made on parts, cohesion held by one idea

The conductor. Other seats perfect one thing; this one owns **the order they happen in** and
**how the whole stays one thing as it grows**.

- **Quality** — how well each part is made. Local. Made by the craft seats.
- **Cohesion (цельность)** — how much the parts feel like *one thing*. Systemic. Held by one
  organizing idea propagated everywhere, defended against entropy as the product grows.

Cohesion is **not the sum of qualities** — perfectly-made screens that each answered their own
question make a patchwork.

---

## The spine: one organizing idea (the concept)

Demand a **concept** before anything else: one *checkable* sentence every decision is measured
against — *"paste any table and it charts itself"*, *"a camera that's always ready"*. It must be:

- **checkable** — you can rule a decision in or out by it;
- **exclusionary** — it says what the product is NOT. Without a border it holds nothing.

With one arbiter, decisions rhyme by themselves: voice, control-grammar, and logic match.
Cohesion is the *consequence* of a shared arbiter, not a separate task (`buro:lebedev` law 10 at
product scale).

---

## The question audit — gate 0 runs on a question nobody has checked yet

Gate 0 asks *what is the real task*. It presumes the question you were handed is the question
worth answering. It usually isn't — and **that goes for the studio's own questions too.** No
question is trusted on arrival.

**An incoming question fails in four ways:**

| Failure | Signature | The move |
|---|---|---|
| **Solution-shaped** | names a fix, not an outcome — "add a filter dropdown", "make it gamified" | recover the outcome behind it. The fix may still be right, but it re-earns its place |
| **Symptom-shaped** | names where it hurts, not what's broken — "the dashboard is ugly", "combat feels off" | trace to cause (`buro:detective` if a quality regressed) |
| **Borrowed jargon** | a term from another product or discipline standing in for the task — "we need a growth loop" | make it re-state the task with the term deleted. If it can't be said without the word, the word was the whole idea |
| **Wrong scope** | asks about one screen when the flow is broken, or about the flow when one control lies | resize, and **name the scope you actually answered** |

**The X-Y check:** they asked how to do X because they believe X gets them Y. Ask what Y is. If X
doesn't reach Y, answering X perfectly is the expensive failure gate 0 exists to prevent.

**The studio's own questions get the same audit.** The question bank below, a seat's gate list, a
brief's checklist — all were written *before anyone saw this material*. **A question whose answer
cannot change what you do next is a ritual, not a question — cut it for this task.** (Same test as
`buro:gamedesign`'s: an option the player always picks isn't a decision.)

**The ritual cut is the most abusable rule in this skill, so it is bounded three ways.** Left
open it stops meaning *don't ask empty questions* and starts meaning *I may skip this*:

- **You must be able to NAME what would have had to change** for the answer to differ, and show
  it didn't. Can't name it → you are not cutting a ritual, you are skipping a check.
- **Never cut a check on the thing you are about to rely on.** A question that guards your next
  action is not a ritual by definition — its whole job is to be asked when the answer looks
  obvious. Cut *checklist* questions; never *verification*.
- **Recency is not validity.** "It's fresh, it was decided two ticks ago" is a claim about the
  clock, not about the inputs. Name what happened in those ticks and why none of it touched the
  decision — or re-ask. State goes stale by *events*, not by *age*.

**And it applies to questions, not to seats.** *"Should I consult X"* is never the kind of
question this rule cuts.

### Three checks that run on the ANSWER (the reframe rule is not self-enforcing)

"Reframe out loud" is a declaration; declarations get skipped under momentum. These three are
mechanical — each is a thing you *do*, and each fails visibly.

**1. Read-back — substitute the answer into their original sentence.** Not your paraphrase: their
words, quoted. If the answer contradicts the literal sentence — its **number, its subject, its
scope** — the reframe is wrong, and the sentence is the evidence.

> *"Introduce a level of item**s** … × weight = price."* Subject: the item. Answer given: *"level
> belongs to the building"* → every item in a building shares one level. Substitute it back and
> the plural becomes meaningless. **One reading and it dies** — but nobody read it back.

**Scope creep hides in a convenient mechanism, not just a wrong noun.** *"Start solo or in a chosen
party"* asked about **grouping**; the answer that shipped — N parallel lanes, each a full copy of
the shared hub — also decided the hub's cardinality, unasked, turning one social space into eight.
"Duplicate the hub" is nowhere in the sentence; it rode along as the cheapest way to satisfy the
part that *was* asked. **Every answer that touches something the question never named is a second,
unaudited decision** — read it back before it ships, not after it's built.

**2. Convenient agreement — when a reading dissolves a problem YOU raised, suspicion goes UP.**
It arrives disguised as luck, and you have a motive to accept it, so you are the worst-placed
auditor of it. Say it out loud — *"this also removes the objection I raised; checking it twice"* —
then check it against the read-back, not against how well it fits.

**This covers reading a RULE, not only a request.** An interpretation of a studio rule that happens
to excuse you from work is the same class, most of all when the rule is one about *economy* (cut
the ritual, don't duplicate, dispatch don't re-carry) — those have a standing motive attached.
**Invoking a rule is not evidence; it is a claim that still needs its check.** Deciding by what the
text permits instead of by what you verified is settling by authority.

**3. Ambiguity is named, never resolved by picking.** *"The level belongs to the building"* has two
readings — **whose** the level is (ownership) and **where** it is assigned (mechanism). They are
not the same claim and they do not imply each other. When a term carries two readings, the answer
**names both and says which it took and why**. A one-line answer to an ambiguous question is the
failure mode itself: the fork is invisible in one line, so a full verdict is not verbosity, it is
what makes the fork visible while it is still cheap.

**Answering a substituted question is not a smaller error than answering wrongly — it is larger**,
because the answer is *correct* and therefore survives critique. The three inward chairs will
polish it; none of them re-reads the request.

**Discipline — this is about the question, never the asker:**
- A wrong question is not evidence of a stupid person. They own the problem, its context, and the
  constraints you can't see; you own the framing craft. Contempt is expensive because it makes you
  stop reading the one input the real question can only be derived from — what they actually said.
- **Reframe out loud.** State the question you are answering and why it differs. **Never silently
  substitute** — a swapped question that goes unannounced is indistinguishable from ignoring the
  request, and it strips the asker of the one call that is genuinely theirs.
- **When the reframe is a guess, deliver both** — the asked-for thing and the re-framed one, with
  the distance between them named.
- If they hear the reframe and keep the original question, **that is their decision**: build it,
  and note the reservation once.

## The prior-art gate — RECON before invention

> The studio has a strong critique machine and a weak **reconnaissance** machine. It breaks what
> has been thought of extremely well, and barely asks whether it needed thinking of.

Every adversarial seat in this studio looks **inward** — at the artifact, its assumptions, its
edges. Nothing looks **outward**, at the field. So the studio's characteristic failure is not a bad
solution: it is **hours spent inventing what the genre settled years ago**, then defended by
critique that never asked the outside question.

**The gate: before inventing anything, name how it is already solved.**

```
1. Name the standing solution — what do the works that already solved this do? Name titles.
   ("Carry weight × item value" — Tarkov, ARC Raiders, every looter.)
2. Name why it stands — what does that solution buy, and what does it cost?
3. Then choose, out loud, ONE of:
   → ADOPT     — take it. Originality is not owed on a solved problem.
   → ADAPT     — take the mechanism, change the parameters, say which and why.
   → DEVIATE   — reject it, and name the specific thing about THIS project the standing
                 solution can't do. Deviation is legitimate; deviation by ignorance is not.
4. If you cannot name the standing solution, you have not earned the right to invent yet.
   Not knowing is a research task, not a licence.
```

**Applies at every invention point** — a new mechanic, a new pattern, a new control, a new
structure, a new pillar in a deepen pass, the generator's opening move at a table. Not once at the
start of the project.

**The trap it prevents is not plagiarism, it's the inverse.** A hand-made rule invented in
ignorance *feels* more original and is usually just worse — it fails at the edges the standing
solution already handles, and it costs the hours the field would have given you for free. Cheap to
run, too: naming the standing solution is minutes; re-deriving it is days.

**Reference is not research.** A title named in the README as an inspiration has *not* been
consulted. Prior art counts only when you can say what the work actually does — the mechanism, not
the vibe. (For games the genre playbooks are `buro:gamedesign` → `references/applied.md`; a seat
whose reference file answers the question and wasn't opened is the gate failing, not passing.)

## The compliance regime — a ban needs a source

The studio has taste about dark patterns. Taste is not a requirement, and stating it as one is
the failure: an unsourced ban cannot be argued with or scoped, so it gets **ignored wholesale**
instead of applied precisely where it is genuinely mandatory. Unsourced is weaker, not stricter.

**So the project declares its regime, once, and it lives in `.buro/active.md` → `## COMPLIANCE`:**
target platforms · target markets/jurisdictions · target age rating · any constraint the author
declares themselves. **Ask for it at gate 0; never assume it.**

Every prohibition anywhere in the studio then lands in one of three tiers, and the tier is stated:

- **BLOCKED** — illegal in a declared market, or prohibited by a declared platform/rating policy.
  Not a taste call. **Undeclared regime → this tier does not exist**, and the seat may not use it.
- **CONDITIONAL** — permitted *if* something is done (disclose the odds, age-gate, label it).
  Name the condition; then it is allowed.
- **TASTE** — nothing requires it; the studio simply thinks it is worse. **Say it once, in one
  line, and the author decides.** Then drop it and do not re-litigate.

⚠️ **Verify, don't remember.** Regulations and platform policies move, and a rule recalled from
training is a TASTE finding wearing a BLOCKED badge. Cite the current text or drop the tier.

This is the honesty law pointed at the studio: *"quality is earned, never extracted"* is the
studio's own arbiter, and applying it to someone else's project **as law rather than as taste**
is itself a form of extraction — taking the author's decision without naming that you took it.

## Where a lesson lives — the studio, not the project

When a tick produces a finding, route it by **what it is about**:

- about **what we're building** → `.buro/active.md` (this project's state);
- about **how we work** — a gate that didn't fire, a seat that missed, a question nobody asked →
  **the studio**, i.e. the seat or this skill. It is a change to the method, and it belongs where
  the *next* project will inherit it.

A process lesson written into the project file is a lesson that will be re-learned from scratch on
the next project. *"The table worked, the question was wrong"* is not a note about this game — it
is a missing chair, and it belongs here.

## Phases & gates (the order is mandatory)

Each phase is a **gate**: don't pass until its question is answered. An accidental skip is the
prime source of expensive rework.

```
PHASE           GOAL                            GATE (don't pass until…)            CALL
-1 Question     the question worth answering    it survived the question audit      this skill (the audit above)
 0 Task         the real job (JTBD)             you can write Who/Does-what/Done    buro:analyst · buro:gorbunov gate 0
 1 Concept      one idea + a border             one checkable sentence + a "not"    buro:analyst (positioning→concept)
 2 Structure    IA, flows, the spine of pillars where's the hero? where's the door? buro:usability
 3 Frame        every screen, every STATE       all states designed, not just happy buro (+ dataviz/exotic)
 4 Craft        type, colour, copy, motion      one grammar; honest; beautiful      buro (+ copy/motion/a11y) → buro:web-designer
 5 Critique     adversarial pass                survived the Skeptic                buro (panel) · buro:usability (consilium)
 6 Iterate      real-use convergence            two clean rounds in a row           this skill (loop below)
```

- **-1 Question** — audit above. The costliest failure is not a bad answer, it is a good answer
  to a question nobody checked.
- **0 Task** — three lines (Who / Does what / Done when), pre-filled from `buro:analyst`'s
  research, not guessed. Skipping it makes everything after a fine answer to the wrong problem.
- **1 Concept** — the one idea + its border, seeded by positioning.
- **2 Structure** — the pillars and how they connect. **One obvious entry** for the undecided AND
  **direct doors** for those who know (both, never one by subtraction). Choose *one surface /
  pages / progressive disclosure / modes / search* **with a reason from the task**. Complexity is
  relocated (Tesler), never amputated.
- **3 Frame** — every screen in **every state** (empty/first-run, loading, error/denied, success,
  ugly edges). Happy-path-only is a third done.
- **4 Craft** — one type system, colour with ≤2 meanings, copy by информационный стиль, motion
  that explains a change; then beauty by craft.
- **5 Critique** — a finding ships only if it survives the Skeptic, and the Skeptic cuts *load*,
  never *capability*.
- **6 Iterate** — below.

The phases are a **default order, not a cage**: loop back when meaning demands (a Phase-5 finding
can reopen Phase 2), but *earn* the jump by naming why.

**Grow & measure run alongside, not after.** Once there's a usable product the loop also engages
`buro:retention`, `buro:growth`, and `buro:experiment`.

**A date is not a gate — readiness is.** When the date and the gate conflict, move the date or cut
scope (`buro:producer`'s cut-list). Never declare a red gate green. (*Cyberpunk 2077*, 2020.)

## Iteration to convergence

```
1. Diagnose    — run the relevant panel/consilium over the current state → verdict.
2. Fix         — apply the highest-leverage cures (relocate load to the system; never amputate).
3. Re-test     — walk the journey as the REAL user in the REAL context; better, a fresh
                 agent or person with no prior context attempts the main job unaided.
4. Re-diagnose — fixes change the friction landscape; a fix can add its own tax. Re-count.
```

**Converged when** a full round surfaces nothing worth changing, a first-timer completes the main
job with no instructions, and every gate is green. **Two clean rounds in a row beat one optimistic
pass.** Iterate **per journey** — frequent, high-stakes journeys earn the most rounds; say which
journeys got how many, so one polished flow doesn't imply a finished product.

**The other failure is not idleness — it is a long, busy, well-recorded grind on one item.** So the
loop carries a **STALL rule**, and it is arithmetic rather than judgement: every item gets an
**acceptance line that can fail** and a **tick budget** when it is ranked; an item whose number has
not *changed* for two ticks, or that is over budget, is **STALLED** — a finding that takes one of
four exits in order (**adopt** the standing solution · **cut** to the honest minimum, ФФФ · **park**
with a revive-if · **escalate carrying the number that failed** and why the first three were
refused), never another attempt at higher effort. Unfalsifiable work — *"make the physics feel
right"* — is the canonical grinder: no instrument, no end. The registers, the state ladder, the
one-state rule between departments, the dev-log/state/decision split and the `.buro/` scaffold are
in **`references/cycle.md`**, and `python3 skills/process/scripts/state_check.py <project>` fails a
project whose registers, stalls or log say otherwise.

**Convergence is measured against the goals**, not against "nothing left to add". Two clean rounds
prove the product is *whole and well-made*, not that it is *the right product*. The terminating
gate carries a third question: **does the world now match the goals it set out to meet** — the
concept's promise and the north-star (`buro:analyst` sets them, `buro:experiment` measures them)?
Name the goal, state the **current distance to it**; only the conductor closes the gate when that
distance is zero. A product can pass every quality check and still miss its goal — that miss is a
finding, not a done-call.

## The completeness loop — concentric top-down passes

> **An empty backlog is not a finished product.** The backlog and the GDD are a *guess made at the
> start*; clearing them means the guess is exhausted, not that the product is complete.

Re-derive the work from the *product's actual state*, never from an emptied list. Each pass
**re-reads the WHOLE product from the top**, one level deeper than the last (does every piece
exist? is it filled? good? fun? polished?), against a **completeness rubric** — not the GDD's own
number, which is a floor, not a cap. Exit only on real convergence: the rubric green, two full
passes in a row surfacing nothing, a fresh player experiencing a whole product, not a shell. **The
agent doing the work is forbidden to declare the project done** — that call belongs to the
conductor, made only on convergence, never because the executor ran out of tasks.

Full mechanics — the pass loop, the completeness rubric, the spiral-of-rounds table, re-review
discipline, the idea-archive, and the tick rule that enforces the done-call — live in
**`references/completeness.md`**. Open it before running a multi-round build; this seat's own
gate list above is general-purpose and doesn't repeat the spiral.

## The state-file — the loop's physical home (`.buro/active.md`)

A loop with no state-file dies at the next context compaction and starts over blind. One file per
project, **re-read at the top of every tick and rewritten at the bottom**. It is **not** the backlog
and **not** the design document: it is the **live state**, regenerated from the product each pass.
⛔ **That is a statement about STATE, and about nothing else.** The design document is not a guess —
it is the **intent**, and it outranks the product on every question of *what must exist and what it
is for*. The product outranks it only on *what is true right now*. (`references/cycle.md` §0) Canonical shape (rename headings per project, keep the parts):

```
# <project> — active state · updated <spiral pass / date>

## Concept         one checkable sentence + its border ("…, NOT …")
## Goals           north-star (buro:analyst) · Goal-distance: <how far the world is, now>
## COMPLIANCE      platforms: <…> · markets: <…> · age rating: <…> · author's own: <…>
                   — the ONLY grounds for a BLOCKED verdict. Undeclared = no BLOCKED tier exists.
## Phase / Round   Phase 0–6 · Spiral R1–R6 (which pass) · Gate: open | blocked by … | CLOSED (converged)
## Backlog         [ ] proposed (R<n>) · [x] done (R<n>)  — an OUTPUT of the sweep, not the source
## RE-REVIEW QUEUE [~] <item> — re-review @ higher bar (R<n>)
## IDEA-ARCHIVE    <idea> — cut: <lens> · revive-if: <condition>
## Composition     <A × B × …> @ arity <n> → promoted | fixed | cut | inert
## TABLE           <what was on it> — <gen → distorter → judge> · R<n> rounds → grew: … · cut: …
## REFRAMES        "<their exact words>" → <what we answered> · read-back: survives | FAILS on <number|subject|scope>
```

**Discipline:**
- **Rewritten by the conductor at the end of every tick.** A `Goal-distance` that never moves is
  itself a finding; an `IDEA-ARCHIVE` entry whose `revive-if` is now met returns to the backlog as
  a candidate.
- **`Gate: CLOSED (converged)` + `Goal-distance: 0` ONLY on the real done-call** — rubric green,
  two clean rounds, a fresh user. Never on an emptied backlog.
- **It is what the enforcement gate reads.** `hooks/buro-done-gate.sh` (Stop hook) reads this file
  and refuses a premature "done" — a prompt-rule can be ignored, a harness hook cannot. Advisory by
  default; hard-block via `BURO_DONE_GATE=block`.

## The composition pass — emergence at any arity (2 → N)

> Quality is made on the parts. **Play is made on their interactions.**

Once there are ≥2 finished elements, put them together and watch what emerges — while **escalating
the number running simultaneously**: pairs → triples → clusters → twenty-plus → the whole system at
once. Some behaviour is **purely multi-element**: absent from every element and every pair, it
appears only at higher counts. So raise the arity deliberately each spiral; the ceiling is
*everything running together*, never "we tested all the pairs."

At **each arity level**, every observed interaction is one of three, each an action:
- **Emergent good** — an undesigned interaction turns out fun or meaningful → **promote it** to a
  first-class element; if big enough, a new pillar.
- **Emergent bad** — a dominant strategy, a dead zone, an incoherence → **fix the seam or cut**
  (the loser goes to the idea-archive with its reason).
- **Inert** — they don't interact at all → **note it**; an element that touches nothing may be
  off-concept.

Judge at **every** level: a combination can be healthy at pairs and break at twenty. Exercised via
`buro:gamedesign` (does it play), `buro:tester` and `buro:chaos` (does it break), balanced via
`buro:experiment`; the promote/fix/cut call is the conductor's.

**Seam:** cohesion-under-growth keeps a new element from *breaking* the grammar; this pass mines
elements for what they *create*.

## The table — several seats in one move

The composition pass collides **elements**. The table collides **seats**. Some material never gets
past *competent* under a single lens: the seat that owns it also owns its blind spot, and its
self-critique is the same eye grading its own homework. Then seat a table — three or four seats
(generator, distorter, judge, optionally a grounder) playing the same material inside one move,
either a **RIFF** (each seat's output is the next seat's input — use it to bring something into
being) or a **FAN** (every seat plays the same material independently, verdicts collided — use it
to judge what already exists). **Generating with FAN produces three sketches and no work; judging
with RIFF contaminates every seat after the first.**

Full mechanics — the three chairs and their typical seats, the riff protocol round by round (the
scout step is the one move that must never be skipped), when a table converges, and the canonical
tables per medium — live in **`references/table.md`**. Open it before actually seating one.

## Cohesion under growth (the entropy problem)

A product falls apart not at launch but at the **hundredth addition** — each new feature arrives
with its own logic, control, and word, and quietly tears the grammar. Gatekeep every entrance:

- **Grammar budget.** Before introducing a new control / pattern / word: does the product already
  have a way to say this? A new element of the language enters **only if no existing one fits.**
- **Every element proves its necessity.** The Skeptic on every addition — *"delete it, what does
  the **user** lose?"* If nothing, it doesn't enter. (User loses, not "screen breaks" — otherwise
  you slide into amputation.)
- **Concept as the growth filter.** A feature passes only if it's **in the concept.** Out-of-concept
  either changes the concept (deliberately, expensively) or doesn't enter. "Good, but not here" is
  a full verdict.
- **Refactor the language, not just the code.** Two controls for one function is cohesion debt —
  collapse to one.
- **When you pull a prop, list what was leaning on it.** A rule invented to patch one hole quietly
  becomes load-bearing: other decisions get made *on top of it*. Replacing it is not a local edit —
  before the swap, enumerate everything that stood on the old rule and re-check each one against the
  new one. What silently kept working is the dangerous kind: it is now standing on nothing.

> Gut-check: take three random screens — *"one product, or three?"* If it wobbles where teams hand
> off, cohesion is leaking along the seams between people.

## Question bank (by phase)

**Question (-1):** Is this question solution-shaped, symptom-shaped, borrowed jargon, or the wrong
scope? · What Y do they believe X gets them? · Which question in this bank cannot change what I do
next — and is therefore a ritual for this task? · If I'm cutting it: can I NAME what would have
had to change, and show it didn't? · Does this interpretation happen to save me work?

**On the answer (every tick):** Does the answer survive substitution into their exact sentence —
number, subject, scope? · Did this reframe dissolve a problem *I* raised (then suspicion goes up,
not down)? · Did the term have two readings, and did I name both or quietly pick one?

**Prior art (before any invention):** How is this already solved, by whom, and what does that
solution buy? · Am I adopting, adapting, or deviating — and if deviating, what specifically about
THIS project can the standing solution not do? · Did I actually consult the work, or only name it?

**Task (0):** Who is this person, in what situation? · What *job* do they hire the product to do, in
their words? · What do they do in the first second? in a month (success)? · Is it the right job? the
right person? not a jargon version of it?

**Concept (1):** One sentence — what is it? Can you *rule a decision out* by it? · What does it NOT
do? · Who's the hero? (If everything matters, nothing does.)

**Structure (2):** Where's the **one obvious entry** for the undecided? · Where are the **direct
doors** for those who know? (Both.) · How many clicks to the goal? dead-ends, loops, duplicate
routes? · One surface / pages / disclosure / modes — **why this** for this person? · If I cut this
"to simplify", what does the **user** lose?

**Screen (3–4):** One function — without "and"? · Where's the hero (~50–70% weight)? · One grammar? ·
All states? · Does each control give instant feedback? · Which control *lies*? · What must they
*learn*? what can be cut? · Read each line aloud — which word is dead weight?

**Growth (always):** Is this feature **in the concept**? · Is there already a way to say this, or am
I adding a new element of the language? · Does it rhyme, or pull away?

**Readiness (5–6):** Does a first-timer complete the journey **with no instructions**? · Did it
survive the Skeptic? · Did the last clean round add anything? · Does hierarchy hold in greyscale?

## How to know it's done (the bar)

Quality — every **part** is clear, honest, and crafted. Cohesion — at the **seams** nothing jars:
one voice, one grammar, one logic, the hero-idea readable through all of it.

> **The interface disappears.** The person thinks about their task — find, buy, understand — and
> never about the product. If they must *learn* the interface, it failed. Cohesion is when that
> disappearance **holds across the whole product**, not on one polished screen.
