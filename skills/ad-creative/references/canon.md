# Ad-Creative — canon

## The hook (first 2 seconds / first glance)
Before the skip button, before the scroll-past, before the eye moves on. No hook, no ad.
Sources of an *earned* hook: a real tension, a genuine surprise, a sharp truth, a pattern
break, a question that itches. **Not** ragebait, fake shock, or a lie. If the hook
over-promises what follows, it betrays.

## Front-loading
Assume they watch 3 seconds / read one line. The single message (from buro:campaign) must
land in that window. Brand and product early, not saved for a payoff most never reach.

## Format-native craft (same idea, re-executed)
- **6s bumper** — one idea, one image, one line; no arc.
- **15–30s spot** — a micro-story with a turn; brand woven, not bolted at the end.
- **Static / OOH** — one visual + ≤7 words; readable at a glance and at distance.
- **Social / carousel** — hook frame first; designed for sound-off; captions.
- Never jam one asset into every slot — the idea travels, the execution adapts.

## Honest demonstration (the honesty gate)
No faked result, no staged before/after the product can't reproduce, no bait-and-switch
between ad and landing page. **What the ad shows, the product does.** A demo that lies is
the fastest trust-killer in marketing.

## Headlines
Specific beats clever. Say the benefit or the tension in the customer's words. Offer 2–3
options with different angles (benefit / curiosity / provocation), each honest.

## CTA
One clear next action; name exactly what happens ("Start free", then the page delivers a
free start). No dark-pattern urgency ("only 2 left!", fake countdowns) unless it is
literally, verifiably true.

## Offer structure (the unit, sized to a page)
The **offer ladder**, in order: problem → demonstration → proof → objections → close. The
order is load-bearing — proof before the ask is evidence, proof after it is defence, and a
close placed before any demonstration asks on credit the page hasn't earned.

**Tier packaging.** Each tier exists to fit a genuinely different buyer, and the line between
them falls on a real difference in the job — not on an arbitrary throttle. The upgrade path
is named: what changes about you that makes the next tier correct. A tier built as a decoy,
to make the middle one look reasonable by contrast, is a lie about the choice being offered.

**Proof placement.** Every claim near the ask must be checkable at the moment it's made.
Proof at the bottom of the page is proof nobody reads; proof next to the claim it supports is
the only proof that does work.

**The honest-objection rule.** Name the strongest objection yourself, on the page, before the
buyer does — then answer it with evidence or concede it plainly. The objection a buyer thinks
of and cannot find addressed is the one that closes the tab, and the seller's silence on it
reads as either not knowing or not saying. Conceding a true limitation costs one sentence and
buys the credibility of everything else on the page.
