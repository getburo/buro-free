# Industrial Design — canon

## Dieter Rams · Ten Principles of Good Design
Good design is:
1. **Innovative** — advances with technology, never novelty for its own sake.
2. **Useful** — usefulness first; drop anything that detracts from it.
3. **Aesthetic** — beauty is part of usefulness; well-made objects affect wellbeing.
4. **Makes a product understandable** — the form clarifies function; at best, self-explanatory.
5. **Unobtrusive** — tools, not decoration; neutral and restrained, leaving room for the user.
6. **Honest** — no promise the product can't keep; no manipulation with false value.
7. **Long-lasting** — never fashionable, so never dated; outlasts throwaway culture.
8. **Thorough to the last detail** — nothing arbitrary; care shows respect for the user.
9. **Environmentally friendly** — conserves resources; minimises physical & visual pollution.
10. **As little design as possible** — *Weniger, aber besser* — less, but better. Back to purity.

## Form follows function (Sullivan) — and its honest reading
Form is not *decorated* function; it *is* function made visible. A shape that fights use
(gloss where grip is needed, a handle no hand fits) has failed regardless of beauty.

## Affordance (Gibson → Norman)
A good physical control **shows how it's operated** without a label: a push-plate reads
push, a knurled ring reads twist, a recess reads insert. If the object needs an instruction
sticker to be used, the affordance failed. Signifiers (a subtle arrow, a texture change)
guide without clutter.

## Ergonomics — the used object, not the render
- Design for the 5th–95th percentile of real bodies; check reach, grip span, weight, thumb
  travel, one-handed use, wet hands, gloves, left-handers.
- Contact points carry the design: where the hand, ear, eye, or body actually meets it.
- Test with a *works-like* prototype in a real hand, not a hero render.

## Silhouette & archetype
Readable in one glance; one coherent object, not a pile of features. Fits the art bible's
shape language (dispatch coherence to buro:art-director).

## Repair, lifespan, sustainability
Design to open (standard fasteners over glue), to replace the wear part, to disassemble for
recycling, to age with dignity. Planned obsolescence is the physical dark pattern — forbidden.

## DFM-lite (hand the depth to buro:manufacturing)
Keep walls uniform-ish, add draft to moulded faces, avoid undercuts you don't need, respect
the process early — a form drawn with no thought to how it's made cannot be tooled.
