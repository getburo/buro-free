# Canon — open-source research & verification

The named sources this seat's method is built from. Credits live here, not in the README.

## The professional standard

**The Berkeley Protocol on Digital Open Source Investigations** (UN Human Rights Office + Human
Rights Center, UC Berkeley School of Law). The reference standard for open-source investigation
conducted to an evidentiary bar. What this seat takes from it:

- **Minimisation as a duty, not a courtesy** — collect what the enquiry needs, and no more. The
  file you keep is part of what you did.
- **Chain of custody** — an item's evidentiary value is a function of what you can say about how it
  reached you. Retrieval date, method, and hash, recorded at collection, not reconstructed later.
- **Preservation before analysis** — archive the artifact first; the live source is not the record.
- **Separating the observed from the inferred** — a finding and an interpretation are written
  differently and never mixed in the same list.
- **The investigator's own security and duty of care** — including to the people who appear
  incidentally in collected material.

## Verification method

**Craig Silverman (ed.), *Verification Handbook*** (European Journalism Centre). The source of the
five-check structure this seat runs at Gate 4: **provenance** (is this the original?), **source**
(who made it?), **date**, **location**, **motivation**. Its central finding is the one this seat
repeats in Lens 8 — the dominant failure is not fabricated material but authentic material
recaptioned.

**Mike Caulfield, SIFT** (Stop · Investigate the source · Find better coverage · Trace claims to
the original). The four-move discipline behind the source ladder. Its contribution is the
insistence on **going up to the origin rather than sideways to more coverage** — more articles is
not more evidence.

**Amnesty International's Citizen Evidence Lab.** Practical verification procedure for
user-generated material, and the case for treating an image's *history* rather than its pixels as
the primary evidence of authenticity (Lens 9).

## Finding

**Bellingcat's online investigation toolkit and case method.** Two things this seat takes: the
**pivot on identifiers** discipline (join records on a registration number, hash or domain — never
on a shared name), and the practice of publishing the method alongside the finding so a reader can
re-run it. Bellingcat's chronolocation and geolocation techniques underlie `verifying.md`.

**Michael Bazzell, *Open Source Intelligence Techniques*.** The systematic survey of where
information lives and how to query it. Used here for `finding.md`'s register ladder — with the
subject scope narrowed hard by `perimeter.md`: this seat researches organisations, products,
claims and records, and the person-centric half of that literature is out of scope by design.

**OCCRP Aleph** and the public corporate-registry ecosystem (national company registers, court
records, procurement portals, standards bodies, patent offices). The point of the register ladder
is that most claims worth checking have a *jurisdiction* that keeps a record of them.

## Information disorder

**Claire Wardle's information-disorder taxonomy** (mis- / dis- / mal-information; the seven types
from satire through fabricated content to false context). Its use here is diagnostic precision:
"false" is not one failure mode, and *false context* — true material, wrong caption — is both the
most common and the least often named. Feeds `traps.md`.

## Why the seat is shaped this way

Each canon above contributes one rule that survives into the method:

| Source | The rule it leaves behind |
|---|---|
| Berkeley Protocol | minimise; preserve before analysing; separate observed from inferred |
| *Verification Handbook* | the five checks; recaptioning beats fabrication |
| SIFT | go **up** to the origin, not sideways to more coverage |
| Citizen Evidence Lab | an artifact's history, not its pixels, is the evidence |
| Bellingcat | pivot on identifiers; publish the method with the finding |
| Bazzell | know which register holds which kind of fact |
| Wardle | name the failure mode precisely; false context is the common one |
