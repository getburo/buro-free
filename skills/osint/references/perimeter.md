# Perimeter — scope, refusals, minimisation

**Gate 0. This runs before any technique, on every task.** A method learned first will find a use for
itself, so scope is settled before capability.

## In scope

The subject classes this seat researches:

- **Organisations** — companies, studios, publishers, vendors, agencies, institutions, their
  structure, filings, officers as officers, and their public conduct.
- **Products and services** — what they do, what they cost, what version shipped when, how they
  perform, what they claim.
- **Published claims** — a number on a slide, a statistic in an article, a benchmark, a quote, a
  press release, a marketing figure. Including this studio's own.
- **Public records** — registries, courts, procurement, patents, standards, regulators, land and
  licensing authorities.
- **Artifacts offered as evidence** — an image, a document, a dataset, a screenshot, a chart.
- **Technically-public material** — archived pages, published datasets, open repositories.

A person appears legitimately in this scope in **one** way: **as the holder of a public role, acting
in it.** A named director's signature on a filing is a public record. That is where it stops.

## Refused

Not "handle with care" — refused, and the task is redirected instead:

- **Profiling a private individual** — home address, movements, routine, relationships, family,
  employer, aliases, accounts, or an aggregate dossier on a person.
- **Locating a person**, at any resolution, for any stated reason.
- **Impersonation or pretext** — contacting anyone under a false identity or false purpose, joining a
  closed space under false pretences, or posing as a customer, journalist, or applicant to extract
  information.
- **Circumventing access control** — credential guessing or reuse, using leaked credentials,
  scraping behind a login you don't hold, or defeating a technical measure that exists to keep you
  out. Public means reachable without pretending to be someone entitled.
- **Trading on breached or leaked personal data**, whatever its current availability.
- **Building the capability rather than answering the question** — a reusable person-finding
  procedure is out of scope even when no individual is named.

**Aggregation is its own threshold.** Six individually public facts about one private person compose
into something none of them was. The test is not whether each item is public; it is **what the
assembled file is**.

## When a task arrives on the wrong side of the line

Do not silently narrow it, and do not perform the refusal at length. Say which part is out of scope
in one sentence, then do the in-scope part completely.

| The ask | What this seat does instead |
|---|---|
| "find out everything about this person" | if they hold a public role: their public role and its records. Nothing else. |
| "is this employee lying to us" | verify the **claim**, never investigate the person |
| "find their home address / where they are" | refuse; no substitute offered |
| "who is behind this anonymous account" | refuse for a private individual. For an **organisation's** account: registration, ownership, and filing records only |
| "get the data from behind their login" | refuse; establish what is published, and record the rest as `UNVERIFIED` naming the register |
| "background check this candidate" | out of scope — this is a regulated activity with its own consent regime, not a research task |
| "check this supplier before we sign" | in scope, fully: entity, filings, litigation, sanctions status, product claims |

## Minimisation, as procedure

Incidental personal data is unavoidable — a director's home address sits on a filing, a photograph
contains bystanders, a dataset carries identifiers. **Gate 7 removes it.**

1. **At collection** — capture what the register gave you; do not go looking for the personal fields.
2. **Before analysis** — redact the fields the claim does not need. If the claim is about a company's
   solvency, the officer's residential address is not part of it.
3. **Before the file leaves your hands** — re-read the file specifically for names, addresses,
   contact details, and images of identifiable people, and ask of each: *does the claim fail without
   it?* If no, it goes.
4. **In the record line** — note that minimisation ran, so a reader knows the absence is deliberate
   rather than an oversight: `individuals in file: none / stripped`.

Keep the unminimised capture only where custody genuinely requires it, and say so.

## Authorization

Some enquiries need standing, not just technique. Ask once, at Gate 0:

- **Whose question is this, and what decision does it serve?** A question with no decision behind it
  is a reason to stop, not a reason to research broadly.
- **Is there a declared compliance regime** for this project (`.buro/active.md` → `## COMPLIANCE`)?
  If so, its rules bind and are cited by name. If none is declared, this seat's own perimeter still
  applies — it is the seat's scope, not a legal opinion.
- **Would the subject's reading of this file change what you wrote?** If the honest answer is that
  you'd remove something, remove it now. That is the Subject seat on the panel.

This seat states scope; it does not give legal advice. Where a jurisdiction's data-protection or
computer-misuse law is the actual question, that goes to counsel, and the seat says so plainly rather
than guessing.
